Mobile UX preview. The screens are the real pages at a real 390×844 viewport — not redrawn mockups.

Add something to the cart on the Storefront tab, then switch to Cart — these are the live pages.

What changed for the phone

The header scrolls away Three rows of pinned chrome is a quarter of a phone viewport held for the whole scroll. It is static below 48rem so the products get the screen. The cart bar takes over the persistent-cart job.
A cart bar in thumb reach Count, running total and a route to the cart, fixed to the bottom. Hidden entirely while the cart is empty, and the footer only reserves room when it is showing, so an empty cart leaves no gap.
Chips scroll instead of wrapping Nine category chips wrapped to four lines and pushed the products below two screens. Now a horizontal snap-scroller, full-bleed to the screen edge with the container padding restored inside.
Price and Add to Cart stack Below 30rem a two-up card is narrower than price and button side by side, so they wrapped unpredictably and the tap target moved around the grid. Stacked, every target is the same size and place.
Search gets its own row Logo, search and cart could not share one row without squeezing the search to a stub. It now sits full-width below the logo, as the reference does.
Single-column strips The trust strip and the footer link columns go to one per row: at two-up, every heading wrapped onto two or three lines and the strip grew taller than the hero copy above it.
Touch behaviour, corrected All fourteen :hover rules moved inside @media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) — touch fakes hover on first tap and leaves it there, so an ungated hover reads as a control stuck down. :active is deliberately ungated: it fires while the finger is down, like a native button. Tap highlight off, inputs at 16px so iOS never zooms, zoom never disabled.
What this preview cannot show. env(safe-area-inset-*) resolves to 0 in a desktop browser, so the page's safe-area padding collapses to nothing here and the home indicator on the frame is decoration, not proof the insets work. The same goes for the removed tap highlight, sticky hover and the URL bar's effect on viewport height — all need real hardware.

Two limits of wrapping file:// pages in frames: the frames are cross-origin to this page, so it cannot script them or read the cart out of them; and in Firefox, which gives each file:// document its own storage, the cart will not carry from the Storefront frame to the Cart frame. Chromium shares one origin, so it does. This preview therefore shows the layout, not the handoff.
